# The Quiet Vale - Thematic Text Style Rule

## Purpose

Thematic text gives the rulebook brief moments of atmosphere without competing with teaching, examples or reference material.

## Content authority

- Use only officially locked copy from the Main Rulebook, Encounter Cards or another approved game-content source.
- Never create, paraphrase or extend lore for the rulebook.
- New rulebook narration may be drafted only when the user explicitly requests it. It becomes an approved source only after the user locks its exact wording.
- A card excerpt may be shortened only by selecting complete, unchanged sentences. Do not use ellipses to disguise an edit.
- Identify reused card copy with its official card type and title.
- Identify approved original rulebook narration with the shared source line `THE QUIET VALE`.
- Thematic text must never contain a rule that is required to play. Removing the passage must leave the teaching complete.

## Visual treatment

- Every thematic passage uses the dedicated flavour frame; it never appears as ordinary body copy.
- Use a restrained pale flax wash and one fine aged-brass cut-corner frame with the exact Encounter-card line flourish centred on its top edge.
- Set the passage in Charter Italic at a retail-readable size. Do not italicise surrounding rules text.
- Set the source line in small Gill Sans uppercase, in brass. It is identification, not a heading in the rules hierarchy.
- Do not use the green numbered-step tabs, rose production-placeholder panels or yellow rules-callout treatment for flavour.
- Do not use quotation marks. The frame and italic typography already establish the change of voice.

## Placement and frequency

- Use three to five moments in a rulebook of this length, including the opening treatment.
- Place at most one flavour frame on a page and use existing quiet space wherever possible.
- Place a frame after a complete teaching thought, never between an instruction and its example, exception or table.
- Keep most passages between 18 and 45 words. The approved opening copy may be longer.
- Distribute passages across the beginning, middle and end rather than clustering them.

## Accessibility and production

- The frame must remain recognisable without colour through border, typography and source-line treatment.
- Maintain strong text contrast and do not reduce the italic passage merely to make it fit.
- Keep the passage and source together as one unbroken object.
- Recheck pagination, widows, clipping and reading order after every placement change.

## Approved section passages

### Building the settlement

*One roof became three. A path reached the workshop, another reached the river, and neighbours carried tools between them. Nobody planned the whole shape alone. The settlement grew wherever one piece of work made the next possible.*

### Strain and Supported

*After the rain, one wall bowed and a path washed thin. Before either failed, neighbours arrived with braces, stone, and dry timber. Nothing grand was done that day. The settlement held because ordinary repairs were made in time.*

### Final scoring

*The result is a board that feels increasingly lived-in. By the end of play, the settlement is not just a score state; it is a record of the group’s choices.*
